02/10Why Vellum

The name is deliberate

Vellum is prepared animal skin, and for over a thousand years it was the material reserved for work meant to last: manuscripts, charters, and texts worth copying by hand at real cost because the knowledge inside them was worth preserving. Paper was cheaper and faster. Vellum was for what mattered.

01

Real knowledge transmission is rare on purpose

A professor who has spent a career on a subject, sitting with a small group of students who can keep pace, has never scaled cheaply. We treat that scarcity as the point, not a problem to design away with bigger classes or recorded lectures.

02

Quality is the constraint, not access

We would rather run fewer seminars, staffed by faculty we can vouch for personally, than expand the catalog with instructors we would not choose for our own children. A course that is not ready does not run.

03

Every design choice protects that scarcity

The application, the fifteen-student cap, the live format, and the option to continue one-on-one all serve the same purpose: keeping what makes a real seminar valuable from being diluted into a generic enrichment product.

03/10Program

Built around a real relationship, not a transaction

Most of what is sold to families in this category is either a generic mentor match or a credential shortcut. Vellum is built around something neither offers: one professor who works with a student from the seminar through an optional Apprenticeship Project, so the relationship deepens instead of resetting with someone new.

01

Real faculty — including experienced industry professionals

Every course is taught live by a university professor, not a teaching assistant or a graduate student. Class size is capped at fifteen students to facilitate genuine participation and engagement.

02

Application-based, not open enrollment

Students apply to join a cohort, including a brief writeup on their college goals. That keeps the room serious and the discussion sharp.

03

A relationship that continues

After the seminar, a student can go further with the same professor through a genuine one-on-one Apprenticeship Project, building on a relationship that already exists rather than a cold mentor match.

04/10What changes for the student

What a Vellum Fellow can do differently afterward

The value here is not another line on an application. It is the difference between a student who has been exposed to a subject and one who has been made to argue about it, in front of someone qualified to push back.

01

Command of a real argument

By the final session, a Vellum Fellow can take an original position and defend it under real pushback from a professor, not recite something they read. That is a different skill from testing well, and it shows in an interview or an essay.

02

An honest first measure of college-level work

The reading load, the pace of discussion, and the standard for original thought mirror an actual university seminar. A student finds out what that level of work feels like before it counts toward a degree, not after.

03

A finished piece of independent work

Every seminar ends in a substantive project the student authored, not attended. It gives them something specific of their own to point to, instead of a vague description of an enrichment program.

05/10Catalog

Seminars built around big questions

Interdisciplinary and question-driven rather than built around a conventional subject silo. Every course ends in a substantive project.

08/10After the seminar

Go from a good seminar to a real project

The seminar alone gives a student a genuine, college-level experience worth having on its own. For those who want to go further, a student can continue one-on-one with the same professor, someone they have already worked with, rather than a stranger assigned for the purpose.

This is where a generic "research program" and a Vellum apprenticeship relationship diverge. There are no gimmicks here, no publishing in an affiliated journal, no manufactured credential. We focus on real project outcomes a student can speak to with substance and highlight in their college applications, supervised closely by a professor who already knows how they think.

By inquiry

Eight hours of dedicated one-on-one time

  • Only open to students who have completed a Vellum course
  • Same professor, building on work already started
  • Ends with an institute-approved certificate of completion
  • Certificate documents the project topic, findings, and the student's own contribution
